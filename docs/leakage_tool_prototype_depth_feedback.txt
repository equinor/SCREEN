Feedback on the leakage-tool prototype inspection
=================================================

The solved network and node connections are very clear and neat. The main
source of confusion was the depth convention in the final plots. The input
CSV files contain physical/reference depths, but `Welldata.get_depths()`
normalizes the geometry to a local zero before the solver paths are built:

	self.depths = self.depths - self.depths[0]

The plotting routines then display the already-normalized path coordinates;
they do not create or restore a mean-sea-level datum. The origin can differ
between wells because zero-valued cement fields are included when the minimum
depth is chosen. This makes the water gap visible in some schematics but not
others.

The absence of plugs is not the controlling factor. In some wells, zero-valued
placeholder fields make the local solver zero coincide with CSV zero. Those
schematics therefore appear to be correctly anchored to mean sea level, but
this is a coincidence of the input placeholders rather than an explicit MSL
coordinate convention.

For interpretation and comparison, zero should consistently represent mean
sea level, or every plot should clearly show both the physical datum and the
local solver offset. The raw/reference depth and normalized solver depth
should remain separate, with the applied transformation included in plot
labels or metadata.

The other difficult point was anchoring CO2 pressure at the bottom. The
current examples appear to build a hydrostatic profile from the top boundary
and then add an offset at the bottom. In CCS containment, the pressure driving
leakage is normally the reservoir pressure, which should be prescribed as the
primary boundary condition and then used to derive the pressure profile.

This interpretation was supported by running `co2_example_complex_flow`. The
pressure at approximately 1000 m was about 18 bar. This is consistent with a
top-anchored pressure of roughly 1 bar, a CO2 hydrostatic increase to about
13 bar over the modeled depth, and the added 5 bar overpressure at the bottom:

	approximately 1 bar + approximately 12-13 bar hydrostatic CO2 pressure
	+ 5 bar overpressure = approximately 18 bar

Thus the example appears to use the top pressure to construct the hydrostatic
profile and then apply the bottom overpressure, rather than prescribing the
reservoir pressure as the primary anchor.

I was able to implement a bottom-anchored case, but the procedure was not
straightforward because it required constructing a custom formation boundary
condition. A simpler API for specifying the bottom reservoir pressure directly
would make the workflow much easier and reduce the risk of inconsistent top,
formation, and bottom pressures. It would also be useful to document clearly
whether each example is top-anchored or reservoir-anchored, and whether the
boundary fluid is CO2 or brine.
